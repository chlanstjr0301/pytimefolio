\claim{\secnum{4.2}{sec:oneshare}\quad Orders under one share round down to zero, so \code{plan()} drops them}

\lead{The server truncates an order's worth in shares to set \code{qty2ord}, so an order smaller than one share's weight asks for 0 shares (\figref{rounding}).} The 4 earlier orders fit truncation and rounding to the nearest share alike: a Samsung Electronics 0.1\% order was worth 4.4 shares at 0.0226\% a share and got 4; the 0.3\% order at 09:15 was worth 13.3 and got 13; a LIG Nex1 0.1\% order was worth 2.3 at 0.0426\% a share and got 2; and the LIG Nex1 order at 08:30 was worth 7.1 and got 7. The floor test separates the two rules. A Samsung Electronics order of 0.04\%, with one share at 0.0227\%, is worth 1.76 shares: rounding to the nearest share gives 2, truncation gives 1. The server set \code{qty2ord} 1 both times it was sent (order ID 1650693 at about 10:26, 1650719 at 10:33:45); each was stopped at once with 0 shares filled, and 1650719 showed the state \code{Accepted} right after placement.

\lead{An order for 0.02\% of Samsung Electronics would therefore be accepted with HTTP 200 and ask for 0 shares.} At 0.0226\% a share it is worth 0.88 shares, which truncates to 0, and it would still use one of the day's orders. \code{plan()} drops such orders before anything is sent.

\lead{One share's weight is the price times 100 divided by the net assets, and the feed reports the net assets directly.} The balance record \code{bs} carries \code{na}, the net assets in KRW: 837,002,427 at 09:52. Every delta carries \code{bs_updated}, which the state merges into \code{bs}, so \code{na} stays current between snapshots: at 10:33 it moved from 844,505,167 to 844,076,987 within 60\,s. Whether \code{na} is the figure the server divides by can be checked against the positions: each holding gives an estimate \code{pos} $\times$ \code{close} $\times$ 100 / \code{wei}, and the median over the 23 holdings at 09:52 was 836,798,658, 0.02\% below \code{na}. The estimate is the noisier of the two because \code{wei} has 2 decimals: for LIG Nex1 at 0.43\%, a rounding of 0.005 moves its estimate by 1.2\%. \code{nav()} therefore returns \code{bs.na} and falls back to the median estimate only when the feed lacks \code{na} (D24). At 09:52 one share weighed 0.0226\% for Samsung Electronics, 0.0426\% for LIG Nex1 and 0.1413\% for SK hynix.

\lead{For a stock the portfolio holds, the price is the feed's \code{close}; for any other stock the caller has to pass one.} The contest server reports no price for a stock outside the portfolio, and market data stays outside the wrapper (N1), so \code{plan()} takes \code{prices=}, a dictionary from ticker to price. Without a price it keeps the order and logs a warning that it could not check it, since dropping an order the wrapper cannot measure would change the strategy's targets on no evidence. Dropped orders are logged one by one and kept in \code{tf.dropped}, so the caller can see what was left out (D22).

\lead{At 09:52 the filter dropped the one order below its share's weight and kept the 2 above.} The targets were the current weights plus 0.03 percentage points (pp) of SK hynix, 0.1~pp of Samsung Electronics and 0.06~pp of Leeno Industrial, with \code{min_wei} lowered from its default of 0.05 to 0.01 so that the 0.03 order reached the filter instead of being skipped as too small. SK hynix +0.03\% was dropped with a warning, since one share was 0.1413\%; Samsung Electronics +0.1\% (one share 0.0226\%) and Leeno Industrial +0.06\% were kept.

\lead{The filter adds no margin.} An order a few percent above one share's weight can still truncate to 0 if the price rises between \code{plan()} and the moment the server computes \code{qty2ord}; on the 09:15 order of \figref{rounding}, the share weight the server used lay between 0.0214\% and 0.0231\%, a range of 8\%. A fixed margin would also drop orders the server sizes at 1 share, so the wrapper leaves the choice to the caller, who can raise \code{min_wei}.

\verdict{the wrapper's \code{plan()} computes one share's weight as price $\times$ 100 / \code{bs.na}, with the price from the feed for a held stock and from \code{prices=} for any other, drops orders below it with a warning and keeps them in \code{tf.dropped}; an order it cannot price is kept with a warning (D20, D22, D24).}
